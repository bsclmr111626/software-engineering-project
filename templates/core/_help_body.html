Methodology

How CodeGuard calculates risk

Every number CodeGuard shows is produced by the rules on this page. Nothing is estimated by an opaque model.

Educational simulation. CodeGuard is not a replacement for a professional security assessment. Scenarios are hypothetical, control effectiveness values are illustrative, and decisions use a simplified deterministic threshold rather than a calibrated prediction.

The weight expresses business criticality and is used only when aggregating stage risks into one overall score.

2. Propagation rules

Each scenario defines directed edges between stages, each with a probability. Normally an edge connects a stage to the next one (forward), but scenarios may also define bypass edges that skip stages and reverse edges that point backwards. A scenario never compromises a stage it has no edge to.

  1. The entry stage is compromised by assumption, with the scenario's base likelihood.
  2. Starting there, each outgoing edge of a compromised stage is evaluated exactly once (breadth-first). A compromised stage is never processed twice, so cycles are safe.
  3. Preventive controls protecting the target stage reduce the edge probability:
effective probability = edge probability × (1 − preventive coverage of target / 100)
  1. If the effective probability is ≥ the threshold (50% by default), the edge propagates and the target becomes compromised.
  2. Otherwise the target is blocked (it would have propagated without controls) or exposed (the probability was below the threshold anyway). Neither propagates further.

The threshold rule makes results reproducible and easy to explain. It is a simplification of conditional likelihood, not a statistically calibrated forecast.

3. Risk scoring

Each stage's likelihood is the likelihood of the most likely successful path from the entry stage (the product of effective probabilities). Stages that were attempted but not compromised keep the likelihood of their strongest attempt.

Raw risk = likelihood × (severity / 5) × (impact / 5)
Residual risk = raw risk × (1 − mitigating coverage / 100)
Combined coverage of independent controls = 1 − ∏(1 − effectivenessᵢ)
Overall risk = Σ(weightₛ × stage riskₛ) / Σ weightₛ
  • Inherent risk is calculated with no controls at all; residual risk with the enabled controls.
  • Preventive controls lower propagation probability (and the entry likelihood). Detective and recovery controls do not stop propagation; they lower the residual risk of stages that are reached.
  • Combining controls multiplicatively assumes they act independently, so coverage can approach but never exceed 100%.
  • The overall score uses a criticality-weighted average rather than the maximum: the maximum is almost always the entry stage, so it would hide the benefit of downstream controls. The peak stage risk is still reported separately.
  • All scores are clamped to 0–100 and rounded half-up to one decimal place.

4. Recommendations and comparisons

For every catalog control that is not enabled, CodeGuard re-runs the same simulation with that control added and reports the exact change in overall residual risk and in the stages that stay compromised. Recommendations are ranked by that modeled reduction, then by relevance to the scenario, effort and priority.

The control effectiveness summary on each report uses the reverse test: it removes each enabled control in turn. Use Rerun with changes and Compare to see before-and-after results side by side. A modeled improvement does not guarantee equivalent protection in a real organization.
